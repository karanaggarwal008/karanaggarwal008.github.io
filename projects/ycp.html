RURAL IMMERSION · 2024–25

Team Drishti & Sweet Chilli

Five days in Lohajimi changed the starting point of our pitch: first understand the problem people actually live with.

The village came before the proposal

At IIM Ranchi’s Young Changemakers Programme, I took part in a five-day rural immersion in Lohajimi, Jharkhand. Our work began with field study: listening to local problems and trying to understand community needs before deciding what to propose.

Crop damage from elephants became the focus of our team’s work. Team Drishti developed Sweet Chilli as a proposed response. We had to connect what we heard in the field with something we could explain and defend to a jury.

Taking Sweet Chilli to the jury

We co-developed and pitched Sweet Chilli to government officials and a jury at the institute, placing among the top three of 17 teams. Presenting the proposal meant turning our field experience into a clear explanation of the problem and our response.

I also represented a labour union in the mock Gram Panchayat. That was another way of approaching a problem from a position different from my own. It made the programme larger than a single competition result.

The outreach work before YCP 4.0

My outreach work began with the third edition of YCP. I reached more than 100 prospective participants and helped secure more than 30 registrations, finishing second on the ambassador leaderboard and receiving the Outreach Champion title. I then applied to participate myself, but was not selected for that edition.

The later YCP 4.0 experience therefore followed both an outreach role and an unsuccessful application. Dev Tiwari mentored Team Drishti during the programme and remained someone I could consult for professional advice, including questions about CFA.

The application before the immersion

Before YCP 4.0, I had worked on outreach for YCP 3.0 and then been rejected as a participant. On my next application I discussed that rejection openly, alongside the work I had continued through Deepshikha and IDMUN. Selection for the later edition came after another quiz and interview, followed by the online Impact Initiator phase.

Our six-person Team Drishti comprised me, Gargi Patankar, Avya Khurana, Prakash Kumar, Vibhuti Patil and Karmishtha Dabral. Dev Tiwari was our mentor. We developed the proposal collaboratively; the team result belongs to all six participants.
