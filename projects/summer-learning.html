ACADEMIC EXPLORATION · 2026

A summer across subjects

Economics, psychology and global business made more sense when I could move between them.

Business strategy at Cornell

In Cornell’s Global Business Strategy pre-college course, I prepared case analyses on the Rob Salamida Company’s export strategy and Intel’s semiconductor manufacturing choices. I completed the course with an A−.

Professor Elena A. Iankova’s feedback on my Intel analysis addressed geographic diversification, risk management and country-selection criteria. I also explored how proximity to the United States could affect coordination. The point was to defend a choice with specific criteria.

Macroeconomics through a second question

In Johns Hopkins’ Elements of Macroeconomics with Dr. Hellen Seshie-Nasser, I used office hours to discuss comparative advantage, banking crises, reserve requirements, quantitative easing and open-market operations. I also identified an issue in an answer key that was corrected.

Those conversations made the course more than material to memorise. They gave me room to ask where an explanation held and what changed when the assumptions changed.

Psychology and public finance

Yale psychology with Professor Yarrow Dunham brought detailed feedback on specificity, cognition and connecting psychological traits with environmental context. Acceptance and Commitment Theory was one of the topics I kept relating to everyday decisions.

Harvard coursework included Public Finance and Money, Markets and Financial Instruments. My Public Finance experience included feedback from teaching assistant Steve White and course material led by Professor Daniel Shoag. Bruce Watson and Rob Munger were part of the Money & Markets course experience. I keep the instructors’ and teaching assistants’ roles distinct.

Where the subjects meet

The courses let me approach the same decision from different directions: incentives in economics, interpretation in psychology and practical trade-offs in strategy. That overlap continues in my research interests.

I do not treat a summer course as a degree from the institution. These were specific learning experiences that sit alongside my current undergraduate study at IIM Bangalore.

The academic conversations behind the institution names

At Johns Hopkins, the recurring subjects in office hours included comparative advantage, long-run aggregate supply, banking crises and central-bank policy. I also raised an answer-key issue that was corrected. At Cornell, my Intel case analysis received an A, while my overall course grade was A−; the feedback focused on the reasoning behind geographic diversification and country selection.

Those details explain why the courses mattered to me. They gave me opportunities to test an interpretation, receive a correction and defend an analytical choice. A list of university names would leave out the part of the experience I actually remember.
