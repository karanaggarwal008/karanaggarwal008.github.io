HARVARD MODEL UN INDIA · 2026 · Bengaluru

The pitch kept going

My presentation depended on a video. Then the video would not play.

A committee problem to work through

At Harvard Model United Nations India’s Bengaluru edition, I represented Nigeria. The committee experience pushed me towards thinking about a workable response to a problem, beyond how a speech would sound.

Anaya Sheth, our committee director, offered feedback and pointers. Caleb Thompson, the Bengaluru chapter’s Director General, was helpful throughout. The people supporting the event affected the experience just as much as the formal sessions.

When the first slide failed

For the business pitch, I had prepared a video with real visuals from cities I had visited in India. It was central to the opening. On stage, the presentation would not open properly, and the video then failed to play through the shared Google Slides setup.

I stepped down to help get the presentation onto the screen, then continued without the video working as planned. I stumbled at points because I had rehearsed around it. Caleb spoke with me backstage afterwards and understood the technical problem behind the presentation.

A useful memory of adaptation

I was a finalist in the Impact Initiative. The result belongs in the record, but the technical failure is also part of the story. It made the limits of rehearsal very concrete: I still had to communicate when the planned opening disappeared.

I came away with another experience of continuing through an imperfect presentation. Preparation matters; so does being able to find the thread again.

The part that rehearsal did not cover

The pitch had been structured around real footage from Indian cities I had visited. When the shared PPTX was displayed through Google Slides, the presentation and video did not work as planned. That meant the opening was not merely missing decoration: a part of the argument I had rehearsed around had disappeared.

I stepped down to help get the presentation on screen and then continued. Caleb Thompson’s backstage feedback mattered because it recognised the technical disruption. I keep that sequence in the story alongside the finalist result; it describes the actual presentation more honestly than a polished result alone.
