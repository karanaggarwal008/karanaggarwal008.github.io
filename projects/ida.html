FOUNDING & DIPLOMACY · August 2023–July 2025

International Debaters Association

A conversation with Durvish became a conference we had to organise ourselves.

From a school-bus conversation

The idea for International Debaters Association began with Durvish Khanduja and me. I developed the concept and began contacting people. We were not stepping into a finished organisation: the conference, the team and the outreach had to be put together.

I handled cold emailing and much of the execution, including partnerships, finance, registration, outreach and conference operations. Durvish handled recruitment and design. Those were different contributions to the same effort.

Making IDMUN happen

International Debaters MUN brought in more than 150 registrations from six countries and generated approximately ₹30,000 in revenue. I supervised a 15-member team across the 2023 and 2024 editions.

The event was associated with the UNHCR MUN Refugee Challenge. I was recognised as a UN Young Champion for Refugees in 2023. The meaningful operational task was still ours: finding participants, answering questions, coordinating people and delivering the conference.

Organising changes how I listen

I had participated in debate and Model UN, but organising made me look at the experience from the other side. Delegates see the sessions. Organisers also see the unanswered email, the registration problem and the person who needs help before the session begins.

That perspective continued into my later MUN work, including chairing, preparing background guides and supporting younger students. At the International Charitable MUN, I evaluated around 30 UNGA delegates and helped with background material and delegate allocation.

Two founders, then an organising team

The founding core was Durvish and me. The larger organising team came later. I developed the idea and handled cold outreach and much of the execution; Durvish recruited people and designed material. Keeping those stages clear matters because a 15-person organising team should not erase the two-person beginning or turn every later contributor into a founder.

My operational work spanned partnerships, registration, outreach, finance and conference delivery. Those responsibilities connected: outreach had to become registrations, registrations had to become a usable delegate experience, and the organising team had to be ready to support that experience. The approximately ₹30,000 figure describes revenue, while the 150-plus figure describes registrations.

From delegate to organiser to chair

My MUN experience developed in several directions. Participating taught me about committee discussion. Founding IDA made me responsible for the conditions in which that discussion happened. Later, evaluating around 30 UNGA delegates at International Charitable MUN involved another kind of judgement: listening to other participants and assessing their work.

Background-guide preparation and delegate allocation were part of that later contribution. The sequence connects the experiences without making them interchangeable. IDMUN was our conference; a chairing or support role at another MUN remained a separate responsibility.
