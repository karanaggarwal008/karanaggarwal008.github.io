VENTURE CONCEPT · 2024–25

GreenStruct

What would construction pollution compliance look like if monitoring, reporting and practical guidance worked together?

Starting with a visible problem

GreenStruct was my venture concept around pollution control at construction sites. The pitch brought together real-time monitoring, reporting, guidance on cleaner materials and a proposed certification approach. The question was how to make compliance easier to understand and manage.

The deck describes sensors for particulate matter and other pollutants, more accessible emissions reporting, and guidance on materials such as low-VOC paints and recycled aggregates. These were proposed features in a venture pitch.

The business model in the deck

I considered subscriptions for monitoring tools and services, alongside consulting fees that would vary with a project’s size and needs. The cost structure included technology development, maintenance, expertise and the work required to assess compliance.

The roadmap moved from product development and a small pilot towards partnerships and possible later expansion. It was useful to map that sequence, even though a roadmap is not evidence that the milestones have been completed.

The Eureka Junior team

The final GreenStruct Eureka Junior team was Karan Aggarwal, Raghav Gadhyan and Manan Agarwal. We reached the finalist group, Top 300 of 81,250 participants, in IIT Bombay’s Eureka Junior competition.

The pitch brought environmental concerns and business-model decisions into the same conversation. It was an early opportunity to explain why a problem could deserve both public attention and an entrepreneurial response.

Who the proposal was designed to serve

The pitch addresses construction sites where pollution monitoring, emissions reporting and material choices need to be handled together. The proposed monitoring layer would track pollutants; the reporting layer would turn those readings into information a project could use; the guidance layer would help a customer consider cleaner materials. The certification idea was another part of that proposed service.

This combination explains why the deck included both subscriptions and consulting fees. Monitoring tools imply an ongoing service, while project-specific advice depends on a site’s size and requirements. Technology development, maintenance and expertise consequently appeared on the cost side of the model.

The sequence in the original deck

The roadmap starts with product development and a small pilot before moving towards partnerships and wider expansion. That order was the way I structured the proposal: first establish that the service works in a limited setting, then consider how it could reach additional customers.

Our Eureka Junior pitch team was Raghav Gadhyan, Manan Agarwal and me. The final-team attribution is specific to GreenStruct. My Techfest campus-ambassador work was a separate activity, and my education initiative with Raghav Batla was EduElevate.
