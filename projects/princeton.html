MENTORING & CASE WORK · June 2025–present

Thinking through a business case

Helping student teams make a case—and continuing to practise making my own.

A mentor’s contribution

I was selected as a startup mentor for Princeton Business Challenge, supporting three to four student teams. I reviewed case solutions and gave practical feedback on how the teams approached the challenge.

The responsibility was to help students improve their reasoning and presentation. Their work remained theirs; mentoring is a contribution to the process rather than ownership of a team’s eventual result.

Still working on my own arguments

I have also continued participating in business case competitions. Preparing a case requires a clear recommendation, an explanation of the assumptions and a way to communicate the trade-offs under a time limit.

That experience informs the feedback I give other students. I know what it feels like to have an idea that sounds convincing until someone asks a specific question about how it would work.

What the mentoring responsibility covered

My selection as a startup mentor drew on prior experience in case competitions. I was assigned to support three to four teams with approaching and solving the challenge. The contribution was guidance and review of their reasoning, rather than replacing their work with my own answer.

That responsibility connects with the other ways I have supported students: individual profile mentoring through the Internship Committee, tutoring through Schoolhouse, and feedback on student writing at school. Each involves responding to someone’s existing work and helping them take the next step.
