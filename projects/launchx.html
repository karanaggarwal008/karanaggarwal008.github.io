LAUNCHX / INNOVATION · Summer 2026

A company brief at LaunchX

Working with MKE Tech turned a broad question about employee efficiency into a team pitch.

Working inside a brief

In LaunchX’s Innovation programme, Nathaniel Macalalad, Davin Phan, Shubh Singhal and I worked with MKE Tech, a US company, on employee efficiency. This was a different starting point from OnPace: there was an organisation and a practical brief to respond to.

We developed the idea and prepared a final presentation with feedback from programme mentors and advisors. The task was to turn our thinking into something another organisation could evaluate.

Feedback before the final pitch

Forest Richter, Ammar Mamdouh, Amina Muslimova, Dr. Carrie Griffin Colbert and Sarah Smith were among the mentors and advisors who supported the programme. Their role belongs alongside the team’s work, rather than being folded into an individual achievement.

Our team received the Best Pitch Award and ranked first on the Innovation Cohort B leaderboard. I received a scholarship to participate in LaunchX, including both this programme and the Online Flagship.

Two useful starting points

The Innovation experience and the Flagship experience gave me two ways to approach building. One began with a company’s problem; the other began with our own proposed product. Both required a team to agree on what mattered and communicate it clearly.

The award recognises the final pitch. It does not mean our proposed solution had already produced measured efficiency gains at the company.

Why the company brief changed the starting point

MKE Tech’s employee-efficiency brief gave us an organisational problem to respond to. Nathaniel, Davin, Shubh and I developed our proposal around that brief, then used mentor and advisor feedback to prepare the final pitch. The work was therefore different from the Flagship programme, where we formed OnPace around our own venture idea.

The Innovation Cohort B team ranked first and received Best Pitch. Forest Richter, Ammar Mamdouh, Amina Muslimova, Dr. Carrie Griffin Colbert and Sarah Smith supported the programme as mentors and advisors. Their feedback helped the development process; they were not members of our four-person student team.
