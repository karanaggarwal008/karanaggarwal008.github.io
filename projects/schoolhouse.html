TUTORING & DIALOGUE · August 2025–present

Schoolhouse.world

Helping someone learn—and making space for people to disagree thoughtfully.

Explaining is a test of understanding

At Schoolhouse.world, I have tutored more than 200 learners from 40 countries across algebra, precalculus, SAT Math, college preparation and related subjects. A learner’s question is useful feedback: it shows where my explanation needs to become clearer.

Tutoring brings people with different educational backgrounds into the same conversation. An approach that feels obvious to one student can be unfamiliar to another, so I have to pay attention to the explanation as well as the answer.

A different kind of classroom

As Dialogues Club President, I oversaw more than 3,000 minutes of student dialogue sessions. The work is not the same as helping someone solve a maths problem. Participants need room to explain a view, hear a different one and remain in the conversation.

I contributed feedback and actual content development to the dialogue curriculum with Kelly and the Northwestern University Litowitz Center for Enlightened Disagreement team. This was practical curriculum work: considering how a prompt or activity would shape the discussion that followed.

Editing alongside teaching

I also served as a staff editor on the blogs team. Writing for learners asks a related question: what does the reader need to understand next? It is another way of helping someone navigate a decision or an unfamiliar process.

Tutoring, dialogue and editing are connected in my experience. In each, the useful outcome depends on attending to someone else’s perspective rather than simply putting more information in front of them.

Curriculum contribution alongside facilitation

My work with Kelly and the Northwestern Dialogues team included both feedback and actual content development. Those are distinct contributions: feedback responds to material already written, while content development helps shape what participants will encounter. I contributed to both, alongside facilitating and supporting dialogue in the community.

I keep that collaborative role attached to the team rather than describing the curriculum as something I produced alone. The programme connected my experience of student discussions with the work of the Northwestern University Litowitz Center for Enlightened Disagreement.

Three kinds of educational work

Tutoring, dialogue and editing gave me three different responsibilities. In tutoring, I helped learners with subjects including algebra, precalculus and SAT Math. In dialogue, the task was to support a discussion in which people could explain and examine differing views. On the blogs team, I worked on written material for readers who might never be present in a live session.

The reported 200-plus learners, 40 countries and 3,000-plus dialogue minutes describe the scale of those activities. I use them together with the actual responsibilities, because the numbers alone do not explain what I was doing. A learner reached, a discussion facilitated and a piece of content edited are different forms of contribution.
