RESEARCH DOSSIER · 2026

From State Primacy to Startup Facilitation

India’s policy evolution, 1956–2025: distinguishing new incentives from the rewriting of existing law.

Publication stage & contribution

Working research

With Basma Ehab Basuoiny and Namratha Chowdary Mogalapu

Three institutional milestones

Our NSRI project compares the Industrial Policy Resolution of 1956, the Income-tax Act of 1961 as amended, and the Income-tax Act of 2025. We examine how the policy environment moved from state-led industrialisation towards explicit startup facilitation.

The key distinction is between introducing a substantive incentive and later consolidating or renumbering it. A replacement statute can change the structure of the text without creating every policy contained within it.

A documentary comparison

The method is comparative documentary analysis informed by institutional theory. The unit of attention is a statutory provision or policy paragraph, considered through its purpose, chronology and relevance to entrepreneurship.

The revised manuscript uses a structured documentary comparison rather than claiming formal multi-coder analysis. It does not estimate the implementation effects of the 2025 Act or attribute earlier startup growth to a later reform.

From hackathon to revision

Our team, Finelytics, ranked 51 among 300 teams at the NSRI Summer Research Hackathon. The research continued through manuscript revision after that result.

Basma Ehab Basuoiny and Namratha Chowdary Mogalapu are co-authors. The current work is a manuscript, with a carefully bounded argument about legal and institutional change. A reduction in legislative chapters is not treated as proof of a matching reduction in compliance costs.

Separating three kinds of policy change

The revised manuscript distinguishes introducing an incentive, modifying an existing incentive and consolidating legislation. This is the organising problem of the comparison. A provision appearing in a replacement Act may continue an earlier policy, so reading only the latest text can misdate the change.

The documentary comparison traces selected instruments across the 1956 industrial-policy framework, the 1961 tax framework and its amendments, and the 2025 replacement legislation. It considers the stated purpose and chronology of provisions alongside their relevance to entrepreneurship. The resulting contribution is a structured account of institutional change.

What the indicators can and cannot answer

The manuscript distinguishes annual recognition certificates from cumulative recognition, recognition from firm formation, and firm formation from survival. It also distinguishes general administrative processing indicators from a startup’s own compliance costs. Those are different variables even when they appear in the same discussion of a business environment.

The proposed mechanisms include clarity, financing incentives, formalisation and policy recognition of entrepreneurship. The study does not identify their causal effects. Future work would need direct evidence on outcomes such as compliance hours, professional fees or firm behaviour, with sufficient observations and an appropriate comparison.
