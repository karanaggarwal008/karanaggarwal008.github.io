RESEARCH DOSSIER · 2026

Geopolitical shocks & Indian aviation

Impact of Short-Duration Geopolitical Events on Indian Stock Market Returns: Evidence from the Aviation Sector.

Publication stage & contribution

Accepted for conference presentation

First author with Dr. Anamika Singh and Vishal Kumar

The sector inside the market

This project examines whether short geopolitical shocks are associated with differential movements in an exposed sector relative to the wider Indian market. The manuscript focuses on the Uri attack in 2016 and Operation Sindoor in 2025.

InterGlobe Aviation, or IndiGo, is the exposed entity; the BSE Sensex provides the market benchmark. The choice brings a sector with travel, fuel and uncertainty exposure into a specific comparison.

Method and interpretation

The analysis uses a difference-in-differences framework, comparing before-and-after movements for the exposed entity and benchmark. The manuscript considers both price-level and return-based specifications.

The reported patterns differ across the two episodes: a positive relative price movement for the Uri period and a negative one for Operation Sindoor. These are manuscript findings for the selected design and windows. They should be read with the assumptions and limitations of the comparison, rather than as a universal rule about conflict and markets.

Authorship and the publication stage

I am first author, with Dr. Anamika Singh and Vishal Kumar. Dr. Singh contributed as finance researcher and mentor through the YRI Fellowship; Vishal Kumar was Research Head and Coordinator.

The paper was accepted for presentation at CEFM 2026, the 4th International Conference on Artificial Intelligence in Economics, Finance & Management at Bharata Mata Institute of Management. The acceptance is for presentation, not a completed journal publication. As of 17 November 2026, the conference is scheduled for 20–21 November.

The comparison inside the model

The empirical design compares the exposed aviation entity and the market benchmark before and after each selected event. The interaction between exposure and the post-event period captures the difference between those changes. The interpretation depends on what would plausibly have happened to the two series without the event, not simply on whether a coefficient has a particular sign.

The manuscript considers both price levels and returns, which answer different versions of the question. It is also important that the selected aviation entity does not stand for every airline. The Uri and Operation Sindoor windows are specific historical episodes; the positive and negative relative movements reported for them are not a general prediction about future geopolitical events.

Authorship and the path to presentation

I conducted this work during high school and am the first and corresponding author, with Dr. Anamika Singh and Vishal Kumar. The conference record includes acceptance for presentation; that is the stage at which I present it here, separate from a published journal article.

The project connects my interest in financial markets with a practical identification problem: deciding what comparison is informative when a major event affects both an exposed business and the broader market. The dossier therefore places the research design alongside the result, rather than presenting the directional findings without their context.
