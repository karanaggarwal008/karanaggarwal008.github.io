Digital finance & SME inclusion

Evidence, mechanisms and future directions in a systematic literature-review manuscript.

Publication stage & contribution

Working research

Solo author

Access is more than a digital channel

This manuscript examines digital finance and financial inclusion for small and medium enterprises through a review of existing literature. The interest is in mechanisms: how a financial technology or delivery channel connects with an enterprise’s ability to obtain and use finance.

It complements my work on vocational lending by approaching a different setting where information, institutional design and access matter.

A review rather than a new field study

The work is a systematic literature-review manuscript. I keep it separate from original survey evidence, an implemented financial product or a completed experiment.

The contribution is a synthesis of evidence and research directions. It remains listed as working research rather than as an accepted journal publication.

Four technologies, several mechanisms

The review organises digital finance into digital payments, fintech lending, mobile money and alternative credit scoring. That separation matters because accepting a payment, accessing a loan and being assessed by a scoring model are different activities. A firm can adopt one without receiving the benefits associated with another.

The manuscript examines information production, transaction costs and network effects. Transaction records can make aspects of a firm’s activity more observable; automation can change the cost of processing small transactions or loans; accumulated data can change a platform’s information advantage. The framework uses these mechanisms to explain why effects may differ across firms and settings.

How the evidence is organised

The working review is structured around technology, setting, outcome and research design. It distinguishes studies of credit access from those of prices, constraints or business outcomes, and separates correlational work from designs intended to support causal inference. That organisation is important when studies use different populations and measures.

The discussion also identifies longer-run outcomes, distributional effects, regulation and complementarities between technologies as research directions. Access is only part of financial inclusion: the terms on which a product is offered and which firms remain excluded also matter. I retain the working-manuscript label while this synthesis develops.
