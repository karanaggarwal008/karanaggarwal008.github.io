AI, progress & uneven adjustment

Artificial Intelligence, Economic Progress, and Uneven Adjustment.

Publication stage & contribution

SSRN preprint

Solo author

Progress is not experienced evenly

This paper grew from economics competition writing into a preprint. I was interested in the gap between broad claims about economic progress and the uneven way an adjustment is experienced by workers and institutions.

The question connects the productivity potential of AI with distribution and transition. An aggregate improvement does not tell the entire story of who adapts, who benefits and who bears the cost of change.

A public working record

The preprint is available on SSRN with DOI 10.2139/ssrn.6154633. I keep the preprint label visible because public circulation is a different publication stage from a peer-reviewed journal article.

My related theoretical work on productivity redistribution has also been developed for journal consideration. Editorial review or external review is a process, not an acceptance result.

How it connects to the rest of my work

The same interest appears in vocational finance and AI hiring governance. In each, a new capability or opportunity creates another question about access, adjustment and institutional responsibility.

I want the argument to remain open to criticism and refinement. The original preprint is linked below so the reasoning can be read in full.
