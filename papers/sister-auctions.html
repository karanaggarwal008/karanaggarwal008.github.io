RESEARCH DOSSIER · 2026

Does starting low help?

Strategic under-pricing, seller reputation and bidder competition in eBay auctions.

Publication stage & contribution

Working research

With Tanishk Garg, Aidana Seitova and Advay Panyala

A familiar tactic with an uncertain mechanism

Our SISTER Economics-track project, with Synthica and Youth Economy Lab, asks whether low opening prices lead to higher final auction prices, and whether seller reputation or bidder competition changes the relationship.

The team was selected among the top five in the YEL Economics track for further publication support. The question was useful because a simple price correlation could be mistaken for evidence that under-pricing itself causes a better result.

Two samples, different purposes

The manuscript uses 628 completed historical eBay auctions across Cartier watches, Palm Pilot M515 PDAs and Xbox consoles for the opening-price and competition analyses. A separate seller-reputation sample began with 200 recently completed auctions and retained 187 after removing missing observations.

Low opening prices are defined relative to a category’s distribution, using bottom-quartile and bottom-decile thresholds. This avoids treating the same nominal opening price as equivalent across very different products.

What the manuscript reports

The manuscript reports a strong positive association between opening and final price, but does not find statistically significant moderation by reputation or bidder competition in the tested specifications. That is not evidence that these factors can never matter.

The samples, observational setting and model choices limit the inference. In particular, sellers choose their opening prices, so the analysis does not establish a causal benefit from setting a low price.

A shared paper and a separate proposal

The authors are Karan Aggarwal, Tanishk Garg, Aidana Seitova and Advay Panyala. The manuscript records equal contributions for Karan, Tanishk and Aidana, with alphabetical surname ordering for those equal contributors.

My individual IARCO proposal develops a related future design. It is separately described below, so the proposed dataset is not confused with the samples used in the four-author manuscript.

Testing the under-pricing claim directly

The detailed opening-price manuscript reports four main specifications for 628 auctions. The opening-bid coefficient is 1.080 in the baseline model and 0.862 after product controls. A specification including bidding activity and duration reports 0.998. The positive association alone does not test the claim that unusually low starts improve the seller’s outcome.

The manuscript therefore tests indicators for the lowest 25% and lowest 10% of opening bids within product category. Their reported coefficients are 0.476 and 15.722, with p-values of 0.990 and 0.720 respectively. Neither test supports a statistically detectable premium from unusually low opening prices in that sample. These numbers belong to the opening-price analysis, distinct from the broader project’s reputation sample.

Why product categories matter

The historical sample contains 136 Cartier-watch auctions, 343 Palm Pilot M515 auctions and 149 Xbox auctions. In the product-specific opening-price models, the reported coefficients are 0.921 for Cartier, −0.002 for Palm Pilot and 0.273 for Xbox. The corresponding p-values are below 0.001, 0.872 and 0.041.

That variation matters to the interpretation. Pooling categories can hide different relationships, and a nominal starting price has a different meaning for a watch and a games console. Category-relative thresholds and category-specific checks help keep those distinctions in view. Seller choice and unobserved item characteristics still prevent these associations from establishing a causal pricing rule.
